## Statement

Identify all unordered pairs of images that originate from the exact same source photograph. Different photographs of the same monument are distinct captures, even when their composition or lighting is similar. Not every image has a duplicate.

At evaluation time, `main.py` must open `test-input.zip` in its current working directory and create `output.csv` in that same directory. The CSV header must be exactly:

```csv
image_a,image_b
```

Each row is an unordered pair of distinct image filenames from the input archive. Repeated or reversed rows earn no additional credit. If no pairs are predicted, the CSV still needs its header. Passing requires F1 >= 0.88; the platform then reveals a flag in the format `SIGMOID_<PHRASE>`.

## Approach

What the training labels show about a duplicate (measured on all 1,399 labelled pairs):

- It is a **resized copy** of its source: 56-99% of the source's width, never the same size.
- The **aspect ratio is kept** (it differs by at most 1.2%).
- About half are a plain resize. The rest are also slightly rotated (up to about 4 degrees), zoomed in a little, blurred or colour-shifted.
- The look-alikes that are *not* duplicates are other photos of the same monument, and other web versions of the same picture (recoloured, cropped differently, or exactly the same size).

`main.py` uses that in two checks for every pair of images:

1. **Shape check.** Two images are compared only if they have a different width and the same aspect ratio (within 1.5%). This keeps 1.1 million of the 8.2 million possible pairs and loses no true pair.
2. **SIFT check.** Each image is converted to grayscale, shrunk to 160x160 with `INTER_AREA` (averaging, so two copies of different sizes shrink to almost the same picture) and described by up to 150 SIFT descriptors. For each descriptor of image A, the brute-force matcher finds the two closest descriptors of image B. The match is good if the closest is clearly closer than the second (distance below 0.75 of it, Lowe's ratio test). The pair is a duplicate if it has at least 45 good matches out of 150.

File names are never used, so shuffled names in the hidden archive do not matter.

### Why 45 good matches

For all 1.1 million pairs that pass the shape check, most true duplicates have 60 or more good matches and almost all other pairs have fewer than 20. Effect of the threshold on the training data:

| Threshold | Accepted | Correct | Wrong | Missed | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1,960 | 1,396 | 564 | 3 | 0.831 |
| 30 | 1,654 | 1,387 | 267 | 12 | 0.909 |
| 45 | 1,409 | 1,351 | 58 | 48 | 0.962 |
| 70 | 1,166 | 1,154 | 12 | 245 | 0.900 |
| 80 | 976 | 969 | 7 | 430 | 0.816 |

Any threshold from about 30 to 70 passes 0.88; 45 gave the best F1, in the middle of that range.

### Known limits

- Most of the 58 wrong pairs are two versions of the same picture that the labels count as different photos (for example a sepia and a black-and-white print of the same old photograph, `img_000091.jpg` and `img_000103.jpg`).
- The settings were chosen on the training data; the hidden test still passed, but its exact F1 is not shown by the platform.
- There is no error handling: an unreadable image, or an image with fewer than 2 SIFT keypoints, would make the script crash. None of the 4,057 training images has either problem.
